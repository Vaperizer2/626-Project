How the scratch CNN classifies a photo

The committed VGG-style network as it runs at inference time. Each box is the tensor at that point, drawn with height for spatial size and width for channel count, so you can watch the image shrink while the description of it grows.

What each stage can learn

Stem and stage 1 see 3 to 12 px: edges, colour bands, texture grain. Stage 2 at 32 px sees component parts: a lead, a stripe, a pin row. Stage 3 at 72 px sees most of a small part. Stage 4 at 136 px sees the whole object and its surroundings. Nothing is pretrained, so every one of these detectors has to be discovered from roughly 2,000 training photos.

Training-only steps not in the picture

During training the crop is a RandomResizedCrop (35 to 100 % of the area), with horizontal flip, RandAugment (2 ops, magnitude 9) and colour jitter 0.2. The loss is cross-entropy with label smoothing 0.1, optimised by AdamW at lr 2e-3, 5 warm-up epochs then cosine decay, fp16 autocast. The checkpoint kept is the epoch with the best validation macro-F1.

The decision rule

Softmax turns the 20 logits into probabilities that sum to 1; the class with the largest probability is the answer. Model A has no abstention threshold, so it always names one of the 20 classes. (Only the deployed SigLIP model abstains below 0.5.) The example above shows the common failure: a resistor photo with inductor as the runner-up, since both are small cylinders with two leads.
